## How to Perform Head Movement in EA SPORTS UFC 5

Slipping punches in the Octagon is the difference between landing a highlight-reel knockout and eating a fight-ending counter. In EA SPORTS UFC 5, head movement is your primary tool for defensive striking, allowing you to slip attacks while staying within range to retaliate. With the right stick, you can guide your fighter’s head in multiple directions to evade strikes and immediately launch counters, but mistiming these movements can leave you vulnerable to massive damage.

To execute head movement, you simply use the right stick on your controller. This mechanic lets your fighter quickly avoid an incoming strike without retreating out of striking range, keeping you close enough to punish opponents who overcommit. Because you maintain your range, good head movement transitions defense directly into offense.

This is supported by the official [EA SPORTS UFC 5 Striking Defense Tutorial](https://www.ea.com/en/games/ufc/ufc-5/tips-and-tricks-hub/how-to-use-head-movement), which confirms the right stick is the key to moving your head in different directions.

## Head Movement Combinations for Quick Counters

Head movement is not just about slipping punches; it is the setup for your own offense. The game links specific head movements to fast counter-striking opportunities, letting you punish opponents the moment they miss.

According to EA’s official guidance, you can use the following head-movement strike combinations to counter quickly:

| Head Movement Direction | Quick Counter Strike |
| :--- | :--- |
| Slip to the right | Left Hook |
| Pull straight back | Straights |

To discover every possible head-movement strike combination available to your created fighter or roster athlete, spend time in Practice Mode. The practice environment is the best place to drill these transitions until they become muscle memory.

## The Risks of Predictable Head Movement

While slipping strikes is essential, EA SPORTS UFC 5 heavily penalizes careless defense. You cannot simply spam head movement or duck constantly, as the game tracks where you move your head in relation to the incoming attack.

Moving your head directly into an opponent's attack increases the damage you take. For example, ducking low into an Uppercut or swaying toward a Roundhouse Kick leaves you in a compromised state. To avoid this, you must read your opponent’s strike patterns.

Avoid falling into predictable patterns with your head movement. If you constantly sway in the same direction, a skilled opponent will bait you into moving your head right into their power strikes. Mix up your defensive motions and stay unpredictable to avoid being countered.

## Practicing Your Defense in the Octagon

Because there are many possible head-movement combinations and severe penalties for mistimed ducks or sways, Practice Mode is your best training ground. Use it to find all possible head-movement strike combinations and to learn the timing required to slip strikes without moving into danger.

Focus on these defensive drills:
- Drill slipping to the right and immediately throwing a Left Hook.
- Practice pulling straight back and firing off Straights.
- Intentionally mix up your slipped directions so you do not become predictable.

By mastering these mechanics, you will turn your striking defense into a potent weapon.
